# History and historical dates

This page is for anyone converting Bahá’í dates before 2015 or checking the dates of
events in Bahá’í history. Before Naw-Rúz 172 B.E. (21 March 2015) there was no single
Badí‘ calendar: Bahá’ís in the West and in Iran reckoned it differently, several
Holy Days were kept on Islamic lunar dates in the East, and some event dates differ
by a day between sources. The [rules](calendar-rules.md) describe the calendar from
2015. The [dataset](data/README.md) gives the Western dates for 1 to 171 B.E., with
a reconstruction of Naw-Rúz in Iran alongside.

Where a source is in Persian, this page summarises it; it does not quote unofficial
translations.

## 1. Two practices before 2015

### In the West

The arrangements for the West were “put in place at the direction of Shoghi
Effendi, as described in the volumes of *The Bahá’í World*” (the Universal House of
Justice, [10 July 2014](https://universalhouseofjustice.bahai.org/activities-bahai-community/20140710_001/)).
The article “Bahá’í Calendar, Festivals and Dates of Historical Significance” appears
in *The Bahá’í World* from volume 4 (1930–32) to volume 20; public copies include
volume 7 on [bahai.works](https://bahai.works/The_Bah%C3%A1%E2%80%99%C3%AD_World) and
[volume XVIII](https://bahai-library.com/bw18_calendar_festivals_dates). It fixed:

| | Gregorian date | Bahá’í date (Naw-Rúz on 21 March) |
|---|---|---|
| Naw-Rúz | 21 March | 1 Bahá |
| Riḍván | 21 April to 2 May | 13 Jalál to 5 Jamál |
| Declaration of the Báb | 23 May | 7 ‘Aẓamat |
| Ascension of Bahá’u’lláh | 29 May | 13 ‘Aẓamat |
| Martyrdom of the Báb | 9 July | 16 Raḥmat |
| Birth of the Báb | 20 October | 5 ‘Ilm |
| Birth of Bahá’u’lláh | 12 November | 9 Qudrat |
| Day of the Covenant | 26 November | 4 Qawl |
| Ascension of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá | 28 November | 6 Qawl |
| Ayyám-i-Há | 26 February to 1 March (4 days, or 5 in a Gregorian leap year) | |
| The Fast | 2 to 20 March | 1 to 19 ‘Alá’ |

The same article says Naw-Rúz falls on 21 March “only if the vernal Equinox precedes
the setting of the sun on that day”, and a letter on Shoghi Effendi’s behalf of
15 May 1940 says the same, adding that the choice of a reference place was for the
Universal House of Justice ([*Bahá’í News* no. 138](https://bahai.works/Bah%C3%A1%E2%80%99%C3%AD_News/Issue_138/Text)).
In practice the West kept 21 March. A United States bulletin of 1967 tied the hours of
the Naw-Rúz Feast to the equinox ([*U.S. Supplement* no. 109](https://bahai.works/U.S._Supplement/Issue_109/Text));
by 1982 the same bulletin said “regardless of the date of the vernal equinox”
([*National Bahá’í Review* no. 118](https://bahai.works/National_Bah%C3%A1%E2%80%99%C3%AD_Review/Issue_118/Text)).
No Western community is known to have kept 20 or 22 March, though that has not been
proven.

**When the West began to use it.** *Bahai News* (later *Star of the West*) began on
21 March 1910 as “Baha No. 1”, numbered by Badí‘ months and printing their Gregorian
first days ([bahai.works](https://bahai.works/Star_of_the_West)). A chronology gives
31 March 1907 for its first use in North America
([bahai-library.com](https://bahai-library.com/chronology/1907-03-31-01)), which this
site has not confirmed. The dataset labels years before 67 B.E. (1910) as
`convention-proleptic`: the Western dates applied to years before anyone in the West
kept them.

### In Iran and the East

- **Naw-Rúz** was the day on which the equinox (*taḥvíl*) fell, by the rule that it
  counts “even should this occur no more than one minute before sunset”
  (Kitáb-i-Aqdas, Questions and Answers 35). The times came from Iranian almanacs,
  and no reference place was ever fixed: a Persian comparative calendar issued with
  the permission of the National Spiritual Assembly of Iran in 1977 says so, and
  contrasts the Bahá’í rule with the Iranian civil rule (the equinox before noon)
  (INBA vol. 95, [Afnan Library](https://afnanlibrary.org/)). Almanac times could
  differ from modern values by 20 to 30 minutes.
- **Four Holy Days were kept on Islamic lunar dates**: the Birth of the Báb
  (1 Muḥarram), the Birth of Bahá’u’lláh (2 Muḥarram), the Declaration of the Báb
  (5 Jamádí I) and the Martyrdom of the Báb (28 Sha‘bán). A letter on Shoghi
  Effendi’s behalf of 10 November 1955 notes that “the Bahá’ís in the East follow the
  Lunar calendar, for certain of the Holy Days” (*Messages to Canada*); see also
  Walbridge, [*Sacred Acts, Sacred Space, Sacred Time*](https://bahai-library.com/docs/w/walbridge_sacred_acts.txt),
  pp. 188–189. Lunar dates move about 11 days earlier each solar year, so these days
  moved through the whole year, and in five Badí‘ years (28, 60, 93, 125 and 158
  B.E.) the Twin Birthdays came twice.
- **Ayyám-i-Há** had whatever length made the year end the day before the next
  Naw-Rúz, as it has everywhere since 2015.
- **Persian chronologies** sometimes give the Iranian civil New Year (1 Farvardín,
  by the before-noon rule) as Naw-Rúz, which is not always the day Bahá’ís kept.
- In 2018 the House of Justice wrote that “the age-old practice in Iran of marking
  the start of the Iranian festival of Naw-Rúz according to the exact moment of the
  occurrence of the vernal equinox is not relevant to the implementation of the
  Badí‘ calendar” (letter on its behalf, 3 May 2018, *Messages from the Universal
  House of Justice 2001–2022*, no. 307).

**The reconstruction in the dataset.** For 1 to 171 B.E. the dataset’s
`eastern_reconstruction` applies the rule used since 2015 (the equinox before sunset
in Ṭihrán) to each year. It gives a Naw-Rúz other than 21 March in 51 of the 171
years (44 on 20 March, 7 on 22 March) and a different length of Ayyám-i-Há in 69. In
1861, 1894, 1927, 1960 and 1993 the equinox fell within 15 minutes of sunset in
Ṭihrán (in 1861, within half a minute), so the reconstruction is marked uncertain.
It is a modern calculation, not a record of what any community observed.

## 2. What changed in 2015

The Universal House of Justice fixed the Holy Days “in accordance with explicit
statements of Bahá’u’lláh, ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, and Shoghi Effendi”, setting aside “certain
discrepancies in the historical record” (10 July 2014).

- **The Declaration and the Martyrdom of the Báb** were fixed on 8 ‘Aẓamat and
  17 Raḥmat. ‘Abdu’l-Bahá had written that these are their Badí‘ dates (the day of
  Kamál of the month of ‘Aẓamat, and the day of Sulṭán of the month of Raḥmat), while
  noting that they were still kept on their lunar dates as in Bahá’u’lláh’s lifetime
  (Tablet quoted in Ishráq-Khávarí, *Ayyám-i-Tis‘ih*, in Persian, [Bahá’í Reference
  Library](https://www.bahai.org/fa/library/)). Under the old Western convention the
  same days were 7 ‘Aẓamat (23 May) and 16 Raḥmat (9 July), so in a year whose
  Naw-Rúz is 21 March they now fall on 24 May and 10 July.
- **The Ascension of Bahá’u’lláh** stayed on 13 ‘Aẓamat, “the seventieth day of
  Naw-Rúz” in ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s words. A letter on behalf of the House of Justice of
  20 December 2017 names the day of the Ascension as one of the discrepancies set
  aside ([Persian](https://www.payamha-iran.org/document/808)).
- **The Twin Holy Days** left 20 October and 12 November in the West, and the lunar
  1 and 2 Muḥarram in the East, for the new-moon rule.
- **Naw-Rúz** in the West moved off a fixed 21 March, and Ayyám-i-Há stopped
  following the Gregorian leap year.

## 3. The epoch

Bahá’u’lláh ruled that “the year sixty A.H. (1844 A.D.), the year of the Declaration
of the Báb, must be regarded as the beginning of the Badí‘ Calendar” (Nabíl, in *The
Bahá’í World*). Year 1 begins at the Naw-Rúz of March 1844, about two months before
the Declaration.

- Nabíl, in the same passage, counted his own Naw-Rúz years from 1845 and calls the
  Naw-Rúz of 1889 “the 46th”, which happens to equal the official year number
  (46 B.E.) because his count followed Hijri years. Treat “the *n*th Naw-Rúz” in early
  Persian sources as possibly one out, and check it against any other date given.
- *The Bahá’í World* volume 4 says the Declaration fell on “the sixty-sixth day after
  Naw-Rúz” in 1844; volume 7 and later say “sixty-fifth”. Day 65 fits a Naw-Rúz on
  20 March 1844, which the before-sunset rule gives, and matches 8 ‘Aẓamat.

## 4. Event dates in the sources

Many Gregorian dates in Bahá’í histories are conversions from Islamic lunar dates.
*The Dawn-Breakers* converts with the Wüstenfeld–Mahler tables; months that began
with the sighting of the crescent could differ from the tables by a day or two, and
the weekday a source gives is often the best check.

| Event | Date in the sources | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Declaration of the Báb | Evening of 22 May 1844, two hours and eleven minutes after sunset; 5 Jamádí I 1260 | The Bahá’í and Islamic day began at that sunset, so it is commemorated on the day labelled 23 May. A letter on Shoghi Effendi’s behalf of 25 November 1936 explains this ([*Lights of Guidance*](https://bahai-library.com/hornby_lights_guidance) no. 1542). “22 or 23 May” is a matter of labelling, not of fact. |
| Martyrdom of the Báb | Noon, 28 Sha‘bán 1266: 9 July 1850 in Bahá’í histories (*The Dawn-Breakers*) | The British consul in Tabríz and Persian court historians give 8 July (Momen, *The Bábí and Bahá’í Religions 1844–1944*, 1981). *The Dawn-Breakers*’ “Sunday” fits neither date by the tables (9 July 1850 was a Tuesday). |
| First day of Riḍván | Afternoon of Wednesday 22 April 1863; 3 Dhi’l-Qa‘dih 1279 (*God Passes By*) | *God Passes By* says “thirty-one days after Naw-Rúz”, which implies Naw-Rúz on 22 March 1863; the equinox in fact fell before dawn on 21 March, which would make 22 April 14 Jalál. The festival is kept on 13 Jalál (Kitáb-i-Aqdas, Questions and Answers 1). |
| Ascension of Bahá’u’lláh | Dawn, 2 Dhi’l-Qa‘dih 1309, 29 May 1892, “eight hours after sunset” (*God Passes By*) | *The Bahá’í World* volume 4 gave 28 May; later volumes give 29 May. Its Badí‘ date, the seventieth day of the year (13 ‘Aẓamat), is not in doubt; the House of Justice set aside the difference in the day (2017). |
| Ascension of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá | Shortly after 1:15 a.m., 28 November 1921 (*God Passes By*) | 6 Qawl in every reckoning. |
| Birth of the Báb | 20 October 1819; 1 Muḥarram 1235 | |
| Birth of Bahá’u’lláh | 12 November 1817; 2 Muḥarram 1233 | |

Sources: [*The Dawn-Breakers*](https://bahai-library.com/books/dawnbreakers/),
[*God Passes By*](https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/shoghi-effendi/god-passes-by/),
*The Bahá’í World* volumes 4 and 7.

## 5. When each Holy Day began

| Holy Day | Event or institution | Known early observance |
|---|---|---|
| Naw-Rúz | Ordained by the Báb in the Persian Bayán | Kept by the Báb and the Bábís from the 1840s |
| Declaration of the Báb | 1844 | Its anniversary is mentioned from 1849, and was kept in Yazd in 1850 |
| Birth of the Báb | 1819 | Celebrated by Ṭáhirih in Karbilá on 30 December 1845 (1 Muḥarram) |
| Birth of Bahá’u’lláh | 1817 | During Bahá’u’lláh’s lifetime; in the West from about 1904 |
| Martyrdom of the Báb | 1850 | Kept during Bahá’u’lláh’s lifetime (Kitáb-i-Aqdas, note 140) |
| Riḍván | 1863 | Proclaimed by Bahá’u’lláh; made the Most Great Festival in the Kitáb-i-Aqdas |
| Ascension of Bahá’u’lláh | 1892 | Added by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá after the Ascension (note 140) |
| Day of the Covenant | Appointed by ‘Abdu’l-Bahá, in place of His own birthday | In the West from 1901 |
| Ascension of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá | 1921 | Listed in *The Bahá’í World* volume 4 (1930–32) |

Sources: the Kitáb-i-Aqdas and its notes; Walbridge, *Sacred Acts, Sacred Space,
Sacred Time*, pp. 217–245 (a secondary source, for the early observances).

**In the dataset**, a Holy Day is not listed before the year of its event (the Day of
the Covenant not before 1892). In the year of the event it has `status: "event"`: the
date is the day of the event, not an anniversary. The Day of the Covenant before 1901
has `status: "proleptic"`. The gem’s `OccasionFactory.first_year` gives the first year
of each Holy Day.

## 6. Converting dates found in sources

- **Islamic lunar dates.** Convert with the tables (the civil epoch, 16 July 622) and
  allow a day or two either way; check any weekday the source gives.
- **“The night of”.** Persian and Arabic name a night by the day that follows it, as
  the Bahá’í calendar does.
- **Julian and Ottoman (Rúmí) dates** are 12 days behind the Gregorian from March 1800
  to February 1900, and 13 days after that. Russian sources often use the Julian
  calendar.
- **Persian solar dates** (from 1925 the official Iranian calendar) are not Badí‘
  dates, even where the months line up.
- **Badí‘ dates on documents.** Shoghi Effendi’s Persian letters, and Iranian Bahá’í
  circulars and periodicals, are dated by Badí‘ month and year, often with a Gregorian
  or solar date beside it. Converting one needs the Naw-Rúz of that year, so prefer
  any equivalent printed on the document.
- **Bábí and Azalí dates** may follow a different convention: the followers of Mírzá
  Yaḥyá placed the intercalary days after ‘Alá’ (Nabíl, in *The Bahá’í World*).
- **Variants in *The Bahá’í World*.** Volume 4 gives 28 May for the Ascension of
  Bahá’u’lláh, calls 26 November the “Fête Day of ‘Abdu’l-Bahá”, puts the Declaration
  on the 66th day, and swaps the 8th and 9th months. Prefer volume 7 or later, and cite
  the volume.
