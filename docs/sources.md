# Sources

Every source cited on this site, grouped by kind. Each has a fixed link
(`sources.html#id`) that the other pages use. All are public. Works in Persian are
summarised on this site, not quoted in unofficial translation.

## The Writings and authoritative texts

- <a id="aqdas"></a>**Bahá’u’lláh, *The Kitáb-i-Aqdas***, with *Questions and Answers*
  and notes. [bahai.org](https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/bahaullah/kitab-i-aqdas/).
  Cited for: Naw-Rúz and the equinox (¶16; Questions and Answers 35); Ayyám-i-Há (¶16,
  note 28); the Fast (¶16, note 25); the day beginning at sunset (note 26); Riḍván
  (Questions and Answers 1); Holy Days in the Fast (Questions and Answers 36); clocks in
  high latitudes (¶10, note 17; Questions and Answers 64); the Holy Days observed in
  Bahá’u’lláh’s and ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s time (note 140).
- <a id="ayyam-i-tisih"></a>**‘Abdu’l-Ḥamíd Ishráq-Khávarí, *Ayyám-i-Tis‘ih*** (“The
  Nine Days”), in Persian: a compilation of Writings on the Holy Days.
  [Bahá’í Reference Library (Persian)](https://www.bahai.org/fa/library/). Cited for:
  ‘Abdu’l-Bahá’s statements that the Declaration and the Martyrdom of the Báb fall on
  the days of Kamál of ‘Aẓamat and Sulṭán of Raḥmat (8 ‘Aẓamat and 17 Raḥmat); that the
  Ascension of Bahá’u’lláh is on the seventieth day of the year; His lists of the nine
  Holy Days; Nabíl’s account of the Ascension, dated in several calendars.

## Shoghi Effendi

- <a id="god-passes-by"></a>**Shoghi Effendi, *God Passes By***, 1944.
  [bahai.org](https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/shoghi-effendi/god-passes-by/).
- <a id="dawn-breakers"></a>**Nabíl-i-A‘ẓam, *The Dawn-Breakers***, translated and
  edited by Shoghi Effendi, 1932. [bahai-library.com](https://bahai-library.com/books/dawnbreakers/).
  Its Islamic-to-Gregorian conversions use the Wüstenfeld–Mahler tables.
- <a id="bahai-world"></a>**“Bahá’í Calendar, Festivals and Dates of Historical
  Significance”**, in *The Bahá’í World*, volumes 4 to 20 (from 1930): the arrangements
  for the West made at Shoghi Effendi’s direction, with Nabíl’s account of the calendar.
  Public copies: [volume 7](https://bahai.works/The_Bah%C3%A1%E2%80%99%C3%AD_World/Volume_7)
  and [volume XVIII](https://bahai-library.com/bw18_calendar_festivals_dates). Volume 4
  differs in places (see [history](history.md#converting-dates-found-in-sources)).
- <a id="letter-1940-05-15"></a>**Letter on behalf of Shoghi Effendi, 15 May 1940**, on
  Naw-Rúz and the equinox, in [*Bahá’í News* no. 138](https://bahai.works/Bah%C3%A1%E2%80%99%C3%AD_News/Issue_138/Text).
- <a id="letter-1955-11-10"></a>**Letter on behalf of Shoghi Effendi, 10 November 1955**,
  on the solar and lunar calendars, in *Messages to Canada*.
- <a id="lights-of-guidance"></a>**Helen Hornby (comp.), *Lights of Guidance***.
  [bahai-library.com](https://bahai-library.com/hornby_lights_guidance). Cited by number:
  no. 372 (Friday as the day of rest); no. 781 (Dublin uses the actual sunset);
  no. 1024 (commemoration hours by the sun); no. 1532 (clock times in high latitudes,
  13 June 1978); nos. 1541–1542 (the hour and day of the Declaration of the Báb).

## The Universal House of Justice

- <a id="uhj-2014-07-10"></a>**Message to the Bahá’ís of the World, 10 July 2014**: the
  decisions that unified the calendar from Naw-Rúz 172 B.E.
  [universalhouseofjustice.bahai.org](https://universalhouseofjustice.bahai.org/activities-bahai-community/20140710_001/).
- <a id="uhj-dates-172-221"></a>**Bahá’í World Centre, *Bahá’í Dates 172 to 221 B.E.***,
  December 2014: the official dates of Naw-Rúz, the Twin Holy Birthdays and Ayyám-i-Há
  (Table A) and of the other Holy Days and Feasts (Table B), prepared “using data provided
  by Her Majesty’s Nautical Almanac Office”, with Ṭihrán’s position from the World
  Geodetic System 1984. [bahai-library.com](https://bahai-library.com/6581).
- <a id="uhj-2014-11-24"></a>**Department of the Secretariat, 24 November 2014**: the new
  moon is the conjunction, not the crescent.
  [bahai-library.com (PDF)](https://bahai-library.com/pdf/uhj/uhj_twin_birthdays_update.pdf).
- <a id="uhj-2017-12-20"></a>**Letter on behalf of the Universal House of Justice,
  20 December 2017**, in Persian: the day of the Ascension of Bahá’u’lláh as one of the
  historical discrepancies set aside; its commemoration at 3 a.m. by the sun.
  [payamha-iran.org](https://www.payamha-iran.org/document/808).
- <a id="uhj-2018-05-03"></a>**Letter on behalf of the Universal House of Justice,
  3 May 2018**: comparing the equinox with sunset in Ṭihrán; Iranian practice is not
  relevant to the Badí‘ calendar. *Messages from the Universal House of Justice
  2001–2022*, no. 307 ([Bahá’í Reference Library](https://www.bahai.org/library/authoritative-texts/the-universal-house-of-justice/messages/)).
- <a id="bahai-org-calendar"></a>**bahai.org, *The Bahá’í Calendar***: the dates for
  172 to 221 B.E. [bahai.org](https://www.bahai.org/action/devotional-life/calendar).

## Periodicals, almanacs and chronologies

- <a id="star-of-the-west"></a>***Bahai News*, later *Star of the West***, from 21 March
  1910 (“Baha No. 1”), numbered by Badí‘ months.
  [bahai.works](https://bahai.works/Star_of_the_West/Volume_1/Issue_1).
- <a id="us-supplement-109"></a>***U.S. Supplement* no. 109** (1967): the Naw-Rúz Feast
  tied to the equinox. [bahai.works](https://bahai.works/U.S._Supplement/Issue_109/Text).
- <a id="nbr-118"></a>***National Bahá’í Review* no. 118** (March 1982): Naw-Rúz on
  21 March “regardless of the date of the vernal equinox”.
  [bahai.works](https://bahai.works/National_Bah%C3%A1%E2%80%99%C3%AD_Review/Issue_118/Text).
- <a id="inba-95"></a>**‘Abdu’l-‘Alí ‘Alá’í, comparative Badí‘ calendar**, reproduced with
  the permission of the National Spiritual Assembly of Iran (1977), in Persian, in
  *Iran National Bahá’í Archives* (INBA) vol. 95. [Afnan Library](https://afnanlibrary.org/).
  The before-sunset rule for Naw-Rúz in Iran, against the civil before-noon rule; no
  reference place yet fixed.
- <a id="taqvim-89"></a>**Persian Bahá’í almanac for 89 B.E. (1932–33)**, approved by the
  National Spiritual Assembly of Iran, in Persian. [Afnan Library](https://afnanlibrary.org/).
  Its equinox times and its lunar Holy Days.
- <a id="ishraq-taqvim"></a>**Ishráq-Khávarí, *Taqvím-i-Táríkh-i-Amr*** (“Chronology of
  the Cause”), 1963, in Persian. [H-Bahai Digital Library](https://www.h-net.org/~bahai/areprint/vol11/).
  Each year headed by its Naw-Rúz.
- <a id="chronology-1907"></a>**bahai-library.com chronology, 31 March 1907**: first use
  of the calendar in North America (citing Stockman; not confirmed here).
  [bahai-library.com](https://bahai-library.com/chronology/1907-03-31-01).

## Astronomy and software

- <a id="meeus"></a>**Jean Meeus, *Astronomical Algorithms***, 2nd edition,
  Willmann-Bell: the equinox (chapters 25 and 27) and new moons (chapter 49) in the
  bahai_date gem.
- <a id="almanac-for-computers"></a>**Nautical Almanac Office, United States Naval
  Observatory, *Almanac for Computers***, 1990: the sunset formula in the gem and on
  this site.
- <a id="nasa-delta-t"></a>**NASA, ΔT**: [polynomial expressions](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEcat5/deltatpoly.html)
  and [uncertainty](https://eclipse.gsfc.nasa.gov/SEhelp/uncertainty2004.html).
- <a id="badidate"></a>**Jan Greis, *badiDate***, a JavaScript library computing the
  calendar from the JPL DE405 ephemeris (to 2200) and VSOP87, for 1844 to 2351.
  [GitHub](https://github.com/janrg/badiDate). The gem is checked against it.
- <a id="bahai-date-gem"></a>**bahai_date**, the Ruby gem this site is generated from.
  [GitHub](https://github.com/lessan/bahai-date), [RubyGems](https://rubygems.org/gems/bahai_date).

## How these were checked

The quotations from public sources on this site were checked against the texts. The
dates in the dataset were checked against Table A of [*Bahá’í Dates 172 to 221
B.E.*](#uhj-dates-172-221), the calendar on [bahai.org](#bahai-org-calendar), and
[badiDate](#badidate), by the gem’s tests. Statements marked as computed were
computed with the gem (its Meeus equinox and new moons, and the *Almanac for Computers*
sunset); where a more precise ephemeris was also used, the page says so.
